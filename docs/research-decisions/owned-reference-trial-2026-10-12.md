# A reference contract before a faster evaluator

Document ID: `reiyah.owned-reference-trial`. Version: `0.1.0`.
12 October 2026. Research decision; no superiority or product claim.

**Use a complete conventional comparison to qualify the next evidence contract.**
Recent methods can reduce the number of labels needed for some statistical
questions. They do not establish which annotation procedure defines the question,
and they do not make an engineering workflow cheaper merely because its
confidence interval uses fewer labels.

## What the frontier review changes

| Method and primary source | Applicable guarantee | Decision for this study |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Paired prediction-powered evaluation, 2608.26638v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.26638v1) | Fixed-time asymptotic inference for paired score differences; oracle variance reduction does not establish the same benefit from a small estimated calibration sample. | Useful comparator when the target is a statistical mean and calibration assumptions are qualified. It does not resolve reference disagreement. |
| [Mani et al., ICML 2026](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/mani26b.html) | Finite-sample analysis shows costs from estimating control-variate coefficients, including possible bias and optimistic intervals with same-sample tuning. | Do not infer a universal finite-budget improvement from an optimal population coefficient. |
| [Prediction-powered active testing, 2607.08347v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08347v1) | Adaptive estimation of a fixed finite-pool loss; reported confidence intervals use asymptotic conditions. | A different contract from exact agreement under every retained reference interpretation. |
| [Weighted sampling without replacement, UAI 2023](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v216/shekhar23a.html) and [finite-population confidence sequences](https://arxiv.org/html/2006.04347v4) | Appropriate sampling and betting conditions can support sequential finite-population inference. | Strong established alternatives for a future qualified sampling problem. Here the supplied references can be enumerated directly. |

The review also inspected prediction-based e-tests, Bayes-assisted intervals,
noisy-judge certification and released implementation details. Their assumptions
remain explicit in the [retained review](../../research/multiorg-reference-admission/0.1.0/methods-review.json).
No reviewed method was reproduced here. This is a targeted decision review, not
proof that every current method was found or that a single technique is best.

## The owned engineering decision

Can Reiyah admit a complete repeated-reference packet without dropping
observations, confusing annotator procedures, or weakening its comparison rules?
If paired inference is qualified, does a detector preference shared by each
recorded whole-cohort procedure survive allowing an imagewise choice among those
references?

[MultiOrg](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.14612v2) supplies three annotation sets on
55 complete microscopy images. The authors already compare models across those
sets using AP and TP/FP/FN/F1. Replacing AP with FP/FN is therefore not a novel
contribution. The specific engineering question here concerns reference-family
composition and the existing Reiyah interface.

These are three procedures from two experts, with shared initial pseudolabels.
The initial reference also informed checkpoint selection. They are neither
independent experts nor authenticated physical truth. An imagewise hybrid is a
declared stress family; it is not an observed third workflow or probabilistic
independence assumption. Microscopy feasibility establishes no automotive transfer.

## Milestone, falsifier and resources

The [first protocol](../../research/multiorg-reference-admission/0.1.0/protocol.json)
froze a complete annotation census and a direct guarded-union admission rule
before annotation acquisition. Its result is retained. A
[separate composition protocol](../../research/multiorg-reference-admission/0.2.0/protocol.json)
was frozen after structural counts, before images, model weights or predictions.
It evaluates each fixed procedure separately and composes the exact results;
it does not raise native limits or retroactively change the first result.

The engineering pilot has a frozen four-image roster and begins with one
complete paired image. The conventional baseline is complete maximum-cardinality
matching followed by ordinary minima, maxima and sums. No exponential brute-force
baseline is needed. Missing tiles, malformed outputs, native-cap refusals or
checker disagreement prevent a complete result. One image cannot test cross-image
coupling; a partial four-image run cannot support the primary endpoint.

Resources are bounded: CPU only, no training or paid services, 600 seconds for the
first paired image, at most 1,200 seconds of inference in this owner, and an
unchanged 70-minute overall owner budget. Acquisition failures and amendments are
retained. Full-source admission, paired inference feasibility and reference
sensitivity are separate acceptance decisions. See the
[study record](../../research/multiorg-reference-admission/0.1.0/README.md) for
executed results and the next concrete dependency.

This study cannot establish the original complete-work value hurdle. That still
requires an actual participating workflow, equal information and correctness
obligations, and prospectively measured human preparation, review, checking and
repair. The declined request for private upstream records does not supply those
inputs or constitute an assessment of Reiyah. Do not reopen that request without
new operator instruction.
