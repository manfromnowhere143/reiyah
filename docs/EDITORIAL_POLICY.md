# Editorial and attribution rules

Document ID: `reiyah.editorial-policy`. Version: `0.1.0`.
Status: operator-directed working rules, 20 September 2026.

These rules record Daniel's instructions for repository presentation. They do
not amend a scientific protocol, accepted artifact or historical result.

## Scope before editing

A request to fix order, wording or a dated update is a request for that edit.
Preserve substantive explanations, diagrams, scope and navigation unless the
operator has authorized their revision. Do not treat a preference for concise
writing as permission to remove most of a research README.

Before a substantial restructure, identify the sections and diagrams to retain,
revise, move or remove, explain the effect on a reader, and establish that the
change fits the requested scope. Explain any broader proposal before editing;
do not infer acceptance from an archive or a successful link check. An already
authorized redesign does not require repeated permission for its routine edits.

Evaluate each diagram by the question it answers, current correctness and
readability. A fixed diagram or word-count target does not establish quality.
Preserve useful explanatory substance on the front page. Moving it to another
file changes its visibility, even when every original byte remains recoverable.

## Reiyah's voice and structure

Write in Daniel's voice: precise, mature, direct, confident in the evidence and
candid about uncertainty. Use concrete problems, methods and findings. Avoid
self-congratulation, generic promotional prose, unsupported novelty or
superiority, inflated adjectives and claims about investor or reader reactions.

Lead with the project and implemented capability. Explain the broader research
mission with its actual status. Keep dated updates after the stable explanation,
methods, evidence and reproduction. Keep current and historical instructions
distinct. Show negative comparisons, corrections and unresolved cases alongside
the relevant positive results.

Do not narrow the mission to a small implementation, or imply the broader
mission is implemented. State which is which. A certificate, test, digest or
second implementation supplies its stated evidence, not physical truth or
independent scientific replication.

## Editorial closeout

Read the latest main and preserve work completed by other sessions. Work in a
fresh owned candidate under the existing repository procedure. Keep the
historical controller and closed candidates unchanged.

Inspect the complete diff. Report substantial additions, removals and moves,
including the before/after section and diagram inventory. Check links and
anchors, render every changed Mermaid diagram and inspect it, verify displayed
numbers against the named retained source, and run appropriate research checks
and the advertised small example. State any unavailable visual verification.
Passing syntax, link or reproduction checks does not establish editorial quality.

Keep the earlier narrative recoverable. Do not move a detailed research result
into a headline without its assumptions. Do not replace the current source
history with an older README wholesale. Commit and push only the reviewed scope
under existing authorization, then read back the exact published bytes.

## Attribution

Preserve Daniel Wahnich as author and committer with the configured human email.
Do not add an assistant, model or provider as author, co-author, contributor,
generated-by credit or session trailer. This applies to commit bodies and merge
messages as well as Git's author and committer fields. Retain truthful research
provenance, actual model/tool use and required third-party attribution.

Before publishing, inspect the raw metadata and complete messages of every new
commit in the proposed push, not only the final commit. An author-name check
alone cannot detect an unwanted `Co-Authored-By` trailer. Verify the same metadata
on readback. Check the exact proposed commit range again if integration changes it.

Historical attribution is a separate question. A new policy or clean commit does
not remove old trailers. Do not claim a contributor disappeared from GitHub
without checking the relevant surface. Rewriting published history changes
commit identities and can invalidate evidence references; ordinary safe-push
authority does not authorize that migration. Preserve a specific finding and
its consequences for operator review before such a change.
