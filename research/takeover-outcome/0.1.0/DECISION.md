# Qualify the outcome before predicting readiness

Document ID: `reiyah.takeover-outcome.decision`. Version: `0.1.0`.
15 October 2026. Exploratory engineering decision; not operator acceptance.

**Add a bounded audit of takeover initiation versus recorded outcome. Keep
predictive readiness, recovery guarantees and causal policy claims unadmitted.**
This advances the HARBOR measurement question after the closed microscopy
comparison. It does not replace that comparison or turn its null result into a win.

The actual Reiyah decision is whether a latency-only observation can substitute
for a recorded outcome in a prospective readiness/recovery benchmark. The full
mission already distinguishes these constructs. What was missing was an audit
of actual paired response and outcome records, rather than another authored
possible-world example. The new channel is a public simulator result field;
its physical validity and sustained recovery interpretation remain unqualified.

The [retained frontier review](../../../docs/research-decisions/frontier-2026-10-12.md)
examines advanced driving models, simulation and human-state studies. Current
primary-source discovery on 15 October identified TD2D as a directly usable
source for this measurement question. Its released records contain both first
maneuver latency and a separate outcome. Recent modeling work does not make
those endpoints interchangeable. This is a targeted refresh, not a comprehensive
state-of-the-art survey or a reproduced comparison with frontier systems.

The alternatives were another perception selector, a physiological readiness
predictor, causal policy estimation, or outcome-channel qualification. The first
would return to closed exposed comparisons. A predictor adds training and sensor
requirements before the target is qualified. Causal estimation additionally
needs a defined intervention, assignment and outcome horizon. The chosen
milestone can expose a measurement failure with the existing CPU and a complete
conventional comparison; no model execution is necessary.

The strongest applicable comparator is an ordinary complete-record census with
an exact SQL self-join. It gets the same fields, corrections and missingness.
The producer uses sorted latency lists and exact rational comparisons. The
separate checker checks all strata and witnesses. These are established methods;
no algorithmic novelty, speed advantage or saved analyst work is proposed.

Before acquisition of individual outcomes, [protocol.json](protocol.json) froze
all 500 expected trials and the within-driver, within-event-type comparison.
A failure with strictly shorter latency than a success is a counterexample to
monotone substitution on these records. Ties stay separate. A zero count would
be a retained null, not proof of safe substitution. The paper's task aggregates
were already exposed. This was retrospective exploration, not a blind study.

The archive directory revealed 500 separate summary members. Before outcome
acquisition, the private plan changed the request allowance from 28 to 540 while
retaining the 64-MiB byte limit, 7,200-second owner limit and 1,800-second closeout
reserve. A transient DNS failure closed the first attempt. One documented
continuation reused captured members; its HTTP429 stop closed acquisition.
No alternate server, archive, participant selection or further retry followed.
The endpoint remains incomplete. See [provenance](provenance.json) for failures,
exposure, costs, correction and custody. Historical active effort is unknown and
is not reset by this owner.

The scientific acceptance boundary is narrow: reproducible recorded
counterexamples can reject deterministic latency substitution. They cannot
qualify physical recovery, infer a causal effect of reacting faster, measure
latent belief or demonstrate a useful product. Outcome provenance, a specified
follow-up horizon and complete opportunity coverage are required before a
recovery benchmark. No new predictor, platform, deployment or paid compute is
justified by this result. Gate A remains operator-unaccepted.
