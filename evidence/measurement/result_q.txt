FINDINGS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE CAMERA AXIS IS CLOSED. FCOS3D, an architecturally distinct second camera detector,
   crossed with both lidars gives 1.107 and 1.072, both excluding 1.0. The camera-lidar
   failure coupling is not an artifact of the Mapillary model. All four cross-modality pairs
   sit in a tight 1.072-1.151 band.

2. THE STRONGEST COUPLING IS LIDAR-LIDAR (1.290). Two lidar detectors fail together most,
   consistent with a shared failure driver they both depend on - lidar point sparsity, the
   same mechanism Results A and B measured (objects with few or zero lidar points).

3. TWO CAMERAS COUPLE LIKE CROSS-MODALITY, NOT LIKE TWO LIDARS. The camera-camera pair is
   1.144, inside the cross-modality band, far below the lidar-lidar 1.290. At the conditional
   level Result H's "same-modality is worse than cross-modality" effect is almost entirely a
   lidar-lidar effect; the camera-camera excess over independence is modest and comparable to
   mixing a camera with a lidar.

REFINED THESIS (corrects the naive "modality diversity necessary but not sufficient")
  The coupling is governed by SHARED FAILURE DRIVERS, not by same-vs-cross modality as such.
  Two lidars share the most (point return), and couple most. Camera-camera and camera-lidar
  share less, and couple less. But every one of the six pairs exceeds independence after five
  admissible confounders, so no pairing - not even two different modalities - reaches the
  independence a redundancy safety argument assumes.
